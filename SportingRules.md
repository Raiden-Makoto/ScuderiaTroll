# Sporting Rules

1. Overtime Shootouts: Controlled pit stop with portable fuel churns and damage repairs allowed—no free tire swaps—guaranteeing a pure green-flag sprint finish.
2. Optional Pit Stops, 3-Swap Cap & Mix-and-Match Compounds: No mandatory pit stops or two-compound rule, and teams can mix different tire compounds across all four wheels on the same car. Drivers get a hard limit of 3 pit-stop tire changes per race (including Red Flags and Safety Cars).
3. True Wet-Weather Racing: No Red Flags for rain or spray alone unless standing water turns the track into an ocean. Switching onto Full Wets is free (doesn't count toward the 3-swap cap), while switching off Full Wets counts as one of your 3 swaps.
4. Common-Sense Track Limits: Anything paved within reasonable lines counts; penalties only apply if leaving the track gains an obvious advantage.
5. Two-Part Engine Penalties: Extra power unit parts dock X Constructors' points and cap that car's race haul below X so winning can't erase the penalty—while leaving the driver's grid spot untouched.
6. 29–26–23 Linear Points System: Every finisher from P1 (23 base) down to P22 (1 base) scores points, with a +6 / +4 / +2 physical podium bonus and +1 for Fastest Lap open to anyone who finishes.
7. Wednesday Night Sprints: Standalone mid-week race on the same track using last week's finishing order as the grid, awarding pure 0/1 points—backed by two penalty-free engine passes per season for Sprint failures.
8. The Leclerc Clause (5-Strike Escalation, Fines, Garage 56 Exile & Grid-Collapse Failsafe): Bad team strategy awards discounted Constructors' base points (higher of finish vs. pre-blunder minus 22 - P up to P11, no podium bonus). Strikes 3–5 carry escalating financial fines alongside: (3) mandatory FIA pit-wall inspection, (4) Constructors' points reset to 0 (or negative points carried into next season), and (5+) Constructor blacklist, moving its drivers into Garage 56 ("Team Independent")—an uncapped neutral sanctuary with FIA pit engineers where drivers fight for the Drivers' Championship while earning 0 Constructors' points. If more than half the grid (>50% of cars) lands in Team Independent, the entire season is immediately canceled.
9. The George Clause (7-Tier Season Stack + Shortcut DSQ + Coach's Challenge Appeals): Strikes stack all season without resetting, and multiple offenses in a single race shortcut to an automatic DSQ (still adding all offenses to the season counter): (1) exact penalty, (2) up to 4× penalty, (3) capped at 1 pt for finishing (0 for DNF), (4) race DSQ, (5) 1-race ban, (6) Drivers' Championship expulsion + 1/6th Constructors' points (min 1 pt if finishing), (7) banned for the rest of the season. Appeals follow NHL Coach's Challenge rules: winning clears the strike, while losing jumps you up an extra tier (e.g., Tier 2 → Tier 4).
10. The Verstappen Clause: The driver always holds the final say on strategy—any pit-wall call can be overridden from the cockpit, and invoking this veto waives Leclerc Clause protection if the driver's own gamble goes wrong.
11. The "Better Driver" Clause: If a demonstrably slower car refuses a team swap order, both drivers' earned points are swapped post-race; if defending the swap causes a crash, the offender gets 0 points and the victim receives $\lfloor \text{Position Points} \times \frac{\text{Laps Completed}}{\text{Total Laps}} \rfloor$ points.
12. The Hamilton Clause: If the FIA / Race Control commits a confirmed regulatory blunder that ruins the competitive integrity of a Grand Prix, the entire race result is voided (0 points awarded to everyone)—ensuring no driver is unfairly punished and no championship is tainted by an officiating mistake.
13. The Medical Clause: If a driver DNFs due to an FIA-verified severe or life-threatening medical emergency in position X, they earn PX points if X ≥ 12, or P11 points (13 pts) if X ≤ 11. Faking or exaggerating a medical emergency triggers 0 points, heavy fines, and automatic race bans.
14. The Intentional Damage Clause ("You Break It, You Buy It" + High-G Safety Escalation): Any collision caused by a driver forces the perpetrator's team to reimburse the victim's repair bills (and absorb the Cost Cap hit)—even for non-DNF damage like broken front wings—while giving the victim a free repair/tire swap that doesn't burn their 3-swap cap. If the hit forces a DNF, the victim also earns \(\min(\text{Current Position Points}, \text{P11 Points [13 pts]})\) and any wrecked restricted parts count against the perpetrator's team's seasonal allowance. If impact forces exceed safe G-force thresholds: verified mechanical failures carry 0 driver punishment, honest driver errors receive moderate/less harsh sanctions, and drivers deemed a "credible threat to safety" face bans up to an indefinite / lifetime suspension.
15. The "Hayes" Anti-Crashgate Clause: Intentionally damaging your own car or scattering debris to force a Safety Car, Red Flag, or Overtime Shootout triggers an automatic DSQ and multi-race/indefinite ban for the driver, 0 Constructors' points + a Leclerc Clause strike/fine for the team, and 0 points for any teammate who gained from the manufactured caution.
16. Championship Tiebreakers: Ties are broken by (1) most P1 finishes, (2) countback on P2–P22 finishes, (3) most recent Grand Prix win, and (4) total Wednesday Sprint points.
