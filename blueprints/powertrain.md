# Powertrain Design
A modified BAE Hybridrive clean diesel-electric powertrain tuned into a parallel/series "55% Diesel / 45% Electric" split running on 100% Renewable HVO (Hydrotreated Vegetable Oil) Synthetic Bio-Diesel with a closed-loop Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR) + Diesel Particulate Filter (DPF) exhaust scrubber. This hits zero harmful exhaust gases (Technical Rule 4) while keeping mechanical torque delivery dominant so we stay legally under the 60% electric cap.

• Variable-Geometry & Compound Turbocharging: Twin electric-assisted turbos (MGU-H)—one mounted cleanly on each outer bank near the exhaust ports. This eliminates long cross-over exhaust plumbing, halves turbo lag, and harvests huge electrical energy from both cylinder banks simultaneously.
• No Fuel-Flow Limit = Massive Boost: Real F1 caps fuel flow to control power. Without that cap, you can run 4.5 to 5.5 bar (65–80 psi) of boost—similar to Audi’s Le Mans R18 e-tron quattro diesels—turning a compact 2.0L–2.4L opposed-piston block into a 750+ hp torque monster all by itself.
• Front-Axle Regen & AWD: With relaxed hybrid rules, you can add a front-axle MGU-K alongside the rear MGU-K. Because a high-boost diesel-electric produces colossal torque out of slow corners, all-wheel-drive traction lets you put all 1,200+ combined horsepower down without shredding the rear tires.
• Active Aerodynamics for Cooling: Since relaxed rules allow active aero, louvers in the sidepods can open automatically under braking and slow corners to dump heat from the intercoolers and batteries, then snap shut on straights for minimum drag.

## Make it Fast

1. Keep It Lightweight (~780–800 kg)

Diesel engines need thick cylinder walls to handle huge compression pressures (200+ bar), which adds weight. To keep the car agile like an F1 car:
• 3D-Printed Titanium & Aluminum-Lithium Block: Use a stressed-member billet block with steel cylinder liners so the engine carries rear suspension loads directly without a heavy subframe.
• Small High-Discharge Battery: Because twin MGU-H e-turbos constantly harvest exhaust energy on straights, you don't need a massive, heavy battery pack—just a 25–30 kg ultra-capacitor or high-power lithium-silicon cell that charges under braking and dumps energy immediately on exit.
• Tiny Fuel Load: At 50%+ thermal efficiency, it finishes a full race on ~65–70 kg of synthetic renewable diesel, saving 30+ kg over conventional race engines.

2. Geared for 370+ km/h (230+ mph)

A 6,200 RPM redline doesn't make a car slow if the gearbox is geared for it:
• Long Final Drive Ratios: Because the OP4 + dual electric motors deliver peak torque almost from idle, you run very tall gears.
• 0–100 km/h (0–62 mph): ~2.1 seconds (thanks to AWD front-axle electric launch).
• 0–300 km/h (0–186 mph): Under 8.5 seconds—rocket-like mid-range pull where electric motors bridge every gearshift seamlessly.
• Top Speed: 375+ km/h (233 mph) on long straights with active aero wings flattened.

3. Extreme Ground-Effect Downforce

Because the flat-4 opposed-piston engine has zero cylinder heads above the block, the top of the engine cover can be sculpted razor-low:
• Unobstructed Rear Wing Airflow: Clean, undisturbed air flows straight over the low engine cover onto the rear wing beam, generating massive rear grip with less drag.
• Blown Diffuser Effect: With relaxed rules, routing the twin turbo exhausts into the edges of the rear diffuser seals the underfloor airflow, creating insane high-speed cornering grip (5G+ lateral forces).

## How to Win
> "Horsepower sells cars, torque wins races" - Shelby
• Corner Exit Domination: While a high-revving conventional F1 engine has to downshift aggressively and keep revs sky-high to stay in its power band, your OP4 diesel-electric delivers 1,200+ Nm of combined system torque right off the apex. With AWD electric traction pulling the front tires and the twin e-turbos keeping boost pinned at 5 bar, it launches out of slow hairpins like a slingshot.
• Effortless Mid-Range Punch: Instead of dancing on the edge of a 15,000 RPM redline, the engine pulls like a freight train from 3,000 to 6,200 RPM, giving the driver massive flexibility to short-shift in wet weather or save tires without losing lap time.
